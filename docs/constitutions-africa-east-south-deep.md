# Constitutions: East and Southern Africa and the Indian Ocean (deep sweep)

Outcome of the deep sweep for 24 states (Ethiopia, Eritrea, Djibouti, Somalia, South Sudan, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, Kenya, Malawi, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Angola, Namibia, Botswana, Lesotho, Eswatini, South Africa, Madagascar, Mauritius, Seychelles, Comoros), read on 2026-10-15/16. Method as in [constitutions-africa-asia-oceania.md](constitutions-africa-asia-oceania.md): a web search only to discover official URLs (restricted to the national domains), then every request to a target host through `PoliteHttp` (identifying User-Agent, robots.txt of the host first and each path checked against it, at least 5 s apart, the host's Crawl-delay when longer), the terms or footer read before any text was stored, and a stop on any refusal. Certificate errors were never bypassed; no mirror, aggregator or proxy was used (Laws.Africa/LII platforms, Constitute Project, WIPO Lex, FAOLEX and UN sites stay rejected).

Loaded: 13 publishers, 16 documents in 7 languages (en 7, fr 3, pt 2, sw, so, rw, mg), **9,755 provisions**. Dev-database run (`migrate`, `sync-catalog`, `ingest` of each source): no ingest error or conflict, `verify-sources` 16/16 pass. Every text is `attested_at_edition`: the edition date is the date the document prints (below), otherwise the retrieval date; where only a month or a year is printed the last day of it is used and the manifest says so. Licence reading: every loaded publisher is AMBER (a generic footer or no terms, an official text, nothing forbidden), `commercial_use: unknown`, so a commercial deployment is refused by the gate; Mauritius is the only publisher with a permission in words (see below). Counsel review is recommended before production use.

## Code and configuration changes

* `ulis/sources/parsers/constitution_text.py` (shared parser, backward compatible; the existing constitution tests pass unchanged): a `crop` hint (a rectangle of the page, for documents printed in parallel columns), also passed through `ulis/sources/parsers/pdf_text.py`; the first-article words `One`, `voalohany` (Malagasy), `mbere` (Kinyarwanda) and the ordinal suffix `aad` (Somali "Qodobka 1aad"); the heading form `N.-(1)` (Tanzania); and a heading style `numspace` ("2 Supremacy of Constitution", Zimbabwe).
* `ulis/jurisdictions/modules/mu.py`: the Mauritius module now also cites the Constitution (`Mauritius Constitution s. 4(1)`) and accepts documents with no judgment extension.
* New: 13 source descriptors and manifests (`scripts/build_constitution_east_south.py` writes them and the 12 blocked descriptors), 11 draft profiles (RW, MG, DJ, AO, SO, TZ, NA, SC, LS, ZW, MZ; MU extended), `tests/test_constitution_east_south*.py` with fixtures in `tests/fixtures/constitution_east_south/`, additive lines in `tests/test_catalog.py`.

## Known gaps

* Several loaded texts are older editions of the Constitution (Lesotho 1993, Mozambique 2004, Zimbabwe 2013, Tanzania 2000/2005, Seychelles 2020, Namibia 2016); the system shows them as attested only at the edition date. Where a newer official text exists only as a scan (Mozambique, Burundi, Comoros) ULIS does no OCR.
* Cut or garbled headings in justified PDFs (Rwanda, Madagascar, Somalia) leave a few articles without their own record: their text stays in the preceding article and the note names the missing numbers.
