# Constitutions, deep sweep: Asia and Oceania

Outcome of the second, deeper sweep for Asia and Oceania, read on 2026-10-16. The first sweep (`docs/constitutions-africa-asia-oceania.md`) allowed about six requests per country and no web search; this one used a web search only to find candidate official addresses (it touches no target host) and then about 20 requests per country at most, every one through `PoliteHttp` (identifying User-Agent, robots.txt read per host and checked per path, at least 5 s between requests, cache under `var/sources/`). The terms were read before a text was stored. A refusal (HTTP 401/403, a firewall page, a reset connection, a script application with no static text) ended the work on that host; nothing was worked around. Research record, not legal advice; counsel review is recommended before production use.

## Result

* Loaded now: 10 countries, 11 constitution documents, 2,767 constitution provisions and 2,562 indexed passages (dev database run: every document ingested without errors or conflicts). New: Bhutan, Fiji, Kazakhstan (ru, kk), Marshall Islands, Mongolia, Philippines, Samoa, Timor-Leste, Turkmenistan, Vanuatu. Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan were `planned` in `docs/constitutions-americas-europe.md` (no terms found); under the AMBER rule of this sweep they are active (that document's two rows are superseded).
* One more source, of Acts: the Acts of Bhutan (`bt-judiciary-acts`): 82 Acts, 9,261 provisions (below). `verify-sources` over the dev database: 93/93 documents pass.
* Recorded as `planned`/`rejected` with the findings: Singapore, India, South Korea, Cambodia, Maldives (planned); Niue, Papua New Guinea (rejected).
* Countries of the region still without a constitution text: India, Singapore, South Korea, Maldives, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Brunei, Malaysia, Afghanistan, North Korea, Tajikistan, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands, Tonga, Kiribati, Tuvalu, Nauru, Palau, Micronesia, Cook Islands, Niue, New Zealand. Main reasons: the official host refuses automated clients (403, firewall, reset), the terms restrict reuse, the text is only on a script application or a scanned PDF, or no official host was found (only PacLII, Constitute and similar).

## Licence reading and method

As in the first sweep. GREEN: the publisher grants reuse in words (none this time). AMBER: no restriction and no permission, only a generic copyright footer or nothing at all; the source is `active` with `commercial_use: unknown` (the gate refuses a commercial deployment). RED: a prohibition, a restriction to personal non-commercial use, an explicit copyright notice on the text, or a refusal; `planned`/`rejected` with a gate test (`tests/test_constitution_asia_oceania.py`). One descriptor per publisher (`config/sources/<id>.yaml`, generated by `scripts/build_constitution_sources_asia.py` from `scripts/constitution_findings_asia_data.py`; the headers quote the publishers). Connector and parser are the shared `constitution_docs` / `constitution_text` pair (the "constitution_pages" of the brief); the minimal `draft_unverified` profiles and the citation module `constitution` are as before.

Parser additions (all backward compatible, covered by tests): `scope_regex` and `part_regex` (sections that restart in every Article: `Philippines Constitution art. III, s. 2`, `art. IX-C, s. 2`); `style: sequence` with `heading_regex` (articles headed by an ordinal in words, Mongolia); the `N.—(1)` heading form of Commonwealth drafting (Fiji); `instrument_type: act` in a manifest document (one work `/bt/act/<slug>` per Act, for a publisher that lists other laws beside its constitution); a PDF whose text layer is private-use glyphs is skipped as `ParseSkipped`.

## Acts of Bhutan (`bt-judiciary-acts`)

The Judiciary of Bhutan's page "Acts" (https://www.judiciary.gov.bt/posts/first-category/acts; robots.txt `Allow: /`, no terms or copyright notice on the home page or the page: AMBER) lists 127 English PDFs. `scripts/build_bt_acts_manifest.py` writes the manifest `config/manifests/bt-judiciary-acts.yaml` from that page (one document per English PDF, title as printed; the page's own link to the Constitution answers 404). The first connector run (127 requests, about 12 minutes including parsing) found 43 links dead (HTTP 404; the Companies Act, the Penal Code amendments, the Tax Act and others) or unreadable (one file `pdftotext` cannot open); the committed manifest lists the 84 that answered. Result of the run on the dev database: 84 documents seen, 82 stored as works `/bt/act/<slug>` (`instrument_type: act`), 2 skipped (the Consumer Protection Act of 2012, whose font maps its letters to private-use glyphs; the Biosafety Act of 2015, a file with no text), 9,261 provisions, 8,653 indexed passages, no errors or conflicts, 83/83 verified. Acts are cited with their own title (`Copyright Act of Bhutan 2001 s. 5`). Structure is recognised from the PDF headings where they allow (sections, chapters, a preamble) and falls back to blocks of paragraphs (the Dzongkha-only amendment Acts are stored as blocks). The page does not say which version of an Act a file is; the retrieval date is the edition date. Size of the committed manifest: 84 documents, 85 requests (about 8 minutes at the crawl delay plus download time), 69 MB of cache, and in the dev database (all twelve sources of this sweep together) 51 MB for about 12,000 provisions.
