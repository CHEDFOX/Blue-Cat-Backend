# Constitutions sweep (deep): Europe, the Caucasus and the Middle East

Read 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-16 with the project's polite client (identifying user agent, robots.txt per host and path, at least 5 seconds between requests to a host). The sources are `config/sources/<id>.yaml` (the full robots.txt, terms and document findings of each publisher) and `config/manifests/<id>.yaml` (the documents fetched). Research aid, not legal advice.

## Result

- Loaded (status `active`): 20 publishers, 31 documents in 23 languages, 5932 stored units (articles, numbered paragraphs, preambles and text blocks) in the development database; 3 publishers with an explicit licence (GREEN: Moldova, Bulgaria, Ireland) and 17 with official text and no stated restriction (AMBER).
- New countries with a profile: 19 (`draft_unverified`); Ireland's existing profile gained the Constitution (0.1.1).
- Read and not loaded: 7 publishers `planned` and 2 `rejected`, each with the publisher's words and a gate test.
- Countries with nothing loaded and no record of their own: 16; 2 further rows are countries already covered elsewhere.

## Method

- Scope: the countries of Europe, the Caucasus and the Middle East whose constitution was not yet loaded. Twelve publishers that the first sweeps had recorded as `planned` (Andorra, Azerbaijan, Belgium, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia, Hungary, Kazakhstan, Lithuania, Moldova, Monaco, North Macedonia, Vatican City State) were read again; the new record replaces the old one. Three more records of the first sweeps (Portugal, Serbia, Türkiye) were read again and stay `planned`, now with the publisher's words.
- Per host, robots.txt was read first and each path checked against it (a group naming the ULIS agent, else `User-agent: *`; longest match). A host that answered 401/403/405/451, a path it disallows, a certificate chain that could not be verified, a bot challenge or a reset connection ended the attempt for that host: no other agent, proxy, mirror or browser was used. Web search was used only to find addresses.
- A source is `active` only when the publisher's own words or a quoted statute allow the use (GREEN) or when the official text carries only a generic footer and no restriction (AMBER: commercial use unknown, so a commercial deployment is refused by the gate). Explicit prohibitions, refusals and unreachable hosts leave the record `planned` or `rejected`.
- Connector `constitution_docs` (manifest-driven HTML or text-layer PDF) and parser `constitution_text`; citation `<Country> Constitution art. N` (module `constitution`; Ireland keeps its own module, which now cites the Constitution the same way). The text is the text of the page or PDF; a document whose structure is not recognised is stored in blocks (`text block N`). Every constitution is attested only as of its edition (the page's own date or the retrieval date).

## Shared code changed

- `constitution_text` parser: `Art. 1er` and `1º`-style ordinals; footnote marks after an article number (`Article 42*[2]`); a one-sentence
  preamble marker is the preamble's text, not its heading; a closing period on a heading line stays with the text; a numword heading
  accepts a dot after the number (`1. gr.`).
- `constitution_docs` connector: a host that closes the connection without answering is recorded in the run's errors and the sweep goes on.
- `constitution` module: `7bis` and `22ter` keep their lower-case suffix in the citation (a single letter is still capitalised).
- `ie` module: validates and cites the Constitution (no Act year or number); `config/jurisdictions/ie.yaml` 0.1.1.

## Reproduce

Run `python -m ulis.cli sync-catalog` and `python -m ulis.cli ingest <source id>` for each source; the connector fetches through the
polite client into `var/sources/<host>/constitutions/` and the cache is reused. `tests/test_constitution_eme.py` checks the descriptors,
the licence gate, the parser on real lines of every document (`tests/fixtures/constitution_eme`) and an ingest-and-search cycle.
